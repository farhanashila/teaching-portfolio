## Course Context: UX Design Prototyping

The **UX Design Prototyping** course at DTU focuses on helping students apply lean prototyping methods for UX design and select suitable tools, methods, and design approaches for different tasks.

The course includes topics such as:

- Identifying user needs and pain points
- Developing a Unique Value Proposition using Lean Canvas
- Modeling user goals, activities, and tasks
- Creating wireframes and storyboards
- Designing microinteractions
- Building Minimum Viable Products through iterative prototyping
- Validating prototypes using hypothesis-driven design
- Applying Design Thinking, Agile, Lean, and Build-Measure-Learn methods
- Considering ethics, bias, scalability, and digital product development

The course combines lectures with hands-on exercises and project-based learning, giving students practical experience in UX prototype development.

Course reference:  
https://lifelonglearning.dtu.dk/en/compute/enkeltfag/ux-design-prototyping-3/

## Teaching Approach

My teaching approach is based on clear communication, practical examples, and constructive feedback. I try to make students feel comfortable asking questions, especially when they are unsure how to move from an idea to a structured prototype.

In UX teaching, I believe it is important to help students understand both the design process and the reasoning behind their decisions. Instead of only pointing out what is missing, I try to explain why a user flow, wireframe, Lean Canvas, or prototype decision matters from the user’s perspective.

My feedback style focuses on:

- Clear and respectful communication
- Actionable suggestions
- Practical examples
- Encouraging iteration
- Helping students connect design choices with user needs
- Supporting both confident and less experienced students
